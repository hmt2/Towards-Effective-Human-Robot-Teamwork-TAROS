A Workshop at TAROS 2026

Towards Effective Human–Robot Teamwork

Exploring how Human–Robot teams are designed and implemented in real-world scenarios.

9 September 2026 · Nancy Rothwell Building, The University of Manchester

Submit a Poster View Schedule

Overview

As robots take on a growing role in aerospace, nuclear, autonomous systems, and healthcare, their success increasingly depends on how well they work alongside people, not just on their technical capability alone. Yet design guidance for Human–Robot teamwork remains fragmented, with lessons learned in one sector rarely reaching another. This workshop brings together researchers and practitioners from across these diverse domains to compare experiences, surface common challenges, and begin building a shared, transferable foundation for designing safe and effective Human–Robot teams.

Through invited talks and poster presentations, the workshop will examine how Human–Robot teams are designed, deployed, and evaluated in practice. A key output will be a catalogue of real-world use cases in Human–Robot teaming, providing a practical foundation for future system design. These discussions will directly inform a whitepaper on Human–Robot teamwork scenarios, which the organisers intend to submit to ACM Transactions on Human-Robot Interaction.

Format

A half-day workshop combining invited talks and poster sessions with corresponding lightning talks, followed by a panel discussion representing different application domains. Panel members will include the invited speaker(s) and invited participants from diverse domains.

Call for Posters

We invite poster contributions on topics including, but not limited to:

  • Requirements engineering for Human–Robot interaction and teaming
  • Evaluation methodologies for Human–Robot teamwork systems
  • Deployment challenges and lessons learned
  • Formal specification and verification of Human–Robot teams
  • Case studies involving Human–Robot teams

Keynote

Andy Weightman

University of Manchester

Title

“Human-robot teams: a symbiotic solution to our problems?”

Abstract

Over the last decade robotics has benefited from advances in many complementary and underpinning technologies including computing power (100-fold), simulation software (GPU accelerated, photo-realistic), energy storage (doubled in MJ/kg), materials (self-healing, soft robotics), sensing (e.g. LiDAR) and artificial intelligence (>1000-fold compute). This has led to the increased prevalence of robotics systems such as UAVs (7-fold increase), robot vacuum cleaners (6-fold increase) and collaborative robots (10-fold increase in value). Are we now entering an age where robots will not just perform tasks humans have traditionally undertaken but instead become co-workers — solving problems such as skills shortages and reductions in risk and economic costs, while improving performance? This talk will review enabling technologies, explore the concept through example applications across industries, and examine the barriers and enablers to adoption in the future.

Bio

Andy is a Professor of Medical Mechatronics at The University of Manchester. His research is challenge driven and focusses on robotics for a safer world and novel technologies to disrupt and transform healthcare. He holds seven active grants spanning advanced manufacturing, robotics for extreme environments, healthcare technologies and a recently awarded NIHR i4i grant for Guided Insertion of Tracheostomy (GIFT), a collaboration with colleagues at Manchester NHS Foundation Trust. He is collaborating with the University of Manchester Innovation Factory to commercialise the GIFT technology, filing patents in five geographical regions. His research has also explored the cause of diabetic foot ulceration, finding differences in thermal regulation between people living with and without the disease. His research group have developed soft robotic insoles to redistribute plantar pressure of the feet when walking to reduce the incidence of ulceration.
